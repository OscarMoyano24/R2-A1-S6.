Future: “Will”, “Be Going To” & “Present Progressive”

In English, we can talk about the future using will, be going to, and the present progressive. Although all three can refer to future events, they are used in distinct communicative situations.

Instant Decision

1. WILL

Decisions made at the moment of speaking, promises & predictions.

“I will go to bed early.”
Prior Plan & Evidence

2. BE GOING TO

Plans decided before speaking or predictions with direct evidence.

“It is going to rain.”
Fixed Arrangement

3. PRESENT PROGRESSIVE

Definite appointments organized with specific time or place.

“I am meeting him at 3 p.m.”
Grammar Module

1. WILL

Will is used mainly for decisions made at the moment, predictions, promises, offers, and future facts.

Affirmative
Subject + will + verb + complement
  • I will study tonight.
  • She will call you tomorrow.
  • They will arrive soon.
Negative
Subject + will not (won't) + verb + complement
  • I won't study tonight.
  • He won't come to the party.
Question
Will + subject + verb + complement?
  • Will you study tonight?
  • Will she come tomorrow?
“I am tired. I will go to bed early.”
The decision is made at the moment of speaking (spontaneous reaction).
Grammar Module

2. BE GOING TO

Be going to is used for plans or intentions that were decided before speaking. It is also used for predictions when there is evidence that something is going to happen.

Affirmative
Subject + am/is/are + going to + verb + complement
  • I am going to study tonight.
  • She is going to buy a new computer.
  • They are going to travel next week.
Negative
Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + verb
  • I am not going to study tonight.
  • He isn't going to play tomorrow.
  • They aren't going to travel this weekend.
Question
Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + verb?
  • Are you going to study tonight?
  • Is she going to travel tomorrow?
“I am going to study tonight because I have an exam tomorrow.”
The plan was decided before speaking.
“Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.”
Prediction with immediate evidence: the clouds.
Grammar Module

3. PRESENT PROGRESSIVE

The present progressive can also be used to talk about the future when we have a definite arrangement or appointment, usually with a specific time or place.

Affirmative
Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing + complement
  • I am meeting my friend tomorrow.
  • She is traveling to Bogotá next Monday.
  • We are having a meeting at 10 a.m.
Negative
Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing
  • I am not working tomorrow.
  • He isn't coming to class.
  • They aren't traveling next week.
Question
Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing + complement?
  • Are you meeting your friend tomorrow?
  • Is she coming to the party?
“I am meeting my professor at 3 p.m. tomorrow.”
This is a future arrangement that has already been organized on the schedule.

Quick Comparison

A concise overview to help you select the exact right future construction instantly.

Form Main Use Example
Will Decision at the moment, prediction, promise I will help you.
Be going to Plan or intention decided before, prediction with evidence I am going to study tonight.
Present Progressive Definite future arrangement (time & place fixed) I am meeting my friend tomorrow.

Easy Way to Remember

Spontaneous
WILL → Decision now
“I'm hungry.” → “I will make a sandwich.”

Decided in the exact moment you felt the sensation.

Intention
GOING TO → Plan already decided
“I have an exam tomorrow.” → “I am going to study tonight.”

The plan existed before this conversation began.

Scheduled
PRESENT PROGRESSIVE → Organized arrangement
“I have an appointment at 3.” → “I am meeting my professor at 3.”

Confirmed with another person and in the agenda.

In Short: The Golden Rule
Will
= I decide now.
Going to
= I already have a plan.
Present Progressive
= I have a fixed arrangement.

Question 1
"The phone is ringing!" — "Don't worry, I ______ it."
Correct! A spontaneous decision made right at the moment of hearing the phone.

Spot on! Prediction based on clear present evidence (the shaking ladder).
Question 3
"I booked the doctor's appointment yesterday. I ______ him tomorrow at 9:00 AM."
Excellent! A definite, pre-scheduled arrangement with a specific time and appointment.

"Why did you buy all this paint?" — "Because I ______ repaint my bedroom this weekend."
Exactly! A prior intention and plan decided before the conversation took place.
